In September 2025 I went to Da Nang for Digital Dragons, the Cybersecurity Challenge 2025, with team BlueCyber_Intern. The contest ran on September 20 and the closing ceremony was on the 21st at VKU. We came home with a consolation prize, and for us that counted as a step forward.

This was not our first Digital Dragons. We had played it in 2024 with two teams and neither did anything special. We still took something from it, mostly a list of what to fix: reading challenges faster, managing time better, and splitting work so each person plays to their strengths. So coming back in 2025, after a year of other contests and a lot of practice together, felt different. We were not here just to try. We wanted to beat our own result from the year before.

## Playing under BlueCyber

Big thanks to BlueCyber for sponsoring us. The company covered the trip so we could just show up and play. I learned most of what I know about malware and threat hunting at BlueCyber, first as an intern and then full time, so playing under the company's name felt right.

## A well-made contest

Digital Dragons is a Jeopardy CTF run by evvolabs and VKU, and this year the challenges were written by CyberJutsu, which showed. For me a good CTF is not about the number of challenges or how hard they are, it is about whether the problems make you actually analyze and reason instead of guess. Some of these needed a solid base, some needed a creative angle, and a few only opened up once we stopped and looked at them from a different side.

That is the part I like about CTF as a way to learn. A hard problem can eat hours and still not budge, but when it finally clicks the thing you learned tends to stick, and even the ones you do not solve are worth going back to afterwards. DDC 2025 had a lot of that.

## A consolation prize that meant something

We finished with a consolation prize. It is not the top of the board, but after a 2024 that produced nothing, coming back with the lineup we trusted most and actually placing felt good. The gap between the two years was not that we suddenly got good overnight. It was a slow build across a lot of contests, a lot of hard challenges, and a fair number of losses, each one pointing at something to work on. A consolation prize is a small line on paper, but for us it was a sign the practice was starting to pay off.

## Da Nang

If I only talked about the challenges and the result I would be leaving out half the trip. After the contest we had time to see Da Nang together, and that is a big part of what I remember. We drove up the Son Tra peninsula, which was the best of it, winding roads and wide views out over the sea, a completely different feeling from sitting in front of a screen picking a challenge apart. The beach was the other half, open and easy after days of focus. None of that helped us solve another flag, but walking around, talking and taking photos is the kind of thing the whole team still smiles about. After being tense together over a problem, seeing a new city together is what makes a team closer.

## A small step on a long road

Digital Dragons 2025 ended with a consolation prize and a trip to Da Nang I will not forget. Next to the international finals it was not the most dramatic contest, but for our team it was a real marker. Two forgettable teams in 2024, a prize in 2025 with the lineup we believed in. Not a huge jump, just a sign that the work was showing. The CTF road does not really have a finish line, so the point is to stay curious, keep practicing, and keep coming back. This was one of the good steps along it.
