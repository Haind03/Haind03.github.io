In July 2025 my team PTIT.BlueCyber flew to Sejong, South Korea for the HackTheon Sejong 2025 final. It is an international student security competition hosted by the city of Sejong, backed by NIS, the Ministry of Science and ICT and KISA, with a 38 million won prize pool. It was my first time leaving the country for a CTF, and we finished 10th.

I had played a fair bit by then, both Jeopardy and Attack-Defense, but all of it at home. This was different. A new country, teams from all over, people we had never met, and no real idea going in how strong the field would be or whether what we had prepared would hold up. That uncertainty was a big part of why the trip felt special. We went to test ourselves and to see how far we could actually get.

First, thanks to PTIT. The school paid for the whole trip and sent us, and we could not have gone otherwise.

## The first days

The organizers looked after the teams really well. A nice hotel, everything arranged, so we could rest and focus on the contest. For a first trip abroad to play CTF, being hosted that carefully was more than I expected.

And the trip was not only the contest room. We had time to sit around together, see a new place, and do the kind of things I had mostly seen online before. After hours of staring at challenges, just talking and resting with the team made everyone a bit closer. That is the quiet upside of an international event. You come for the CTF, but a lot of what you take home is the rest of it.

## The last half hour

The final ran on July 10, from 10:00 to 18:00. The morning went well and at one point we were 4th. Seeing our name near the top of an international board was hard to describe, and with about half an hour left we genuinely thought we could hold a prize spot.

But a CTF scoreboard moves fast in the last stretch, when every team is still solving. Sitting high at one moment guarantees nothing about where you finish. Through the afternoon other teams kept scoring and we slid down, one place at a time. Our last flag went in at 17:31:40 for 3,764 points, and by the time the clock stopped we were out of the prizes.

![The final scoreboard of HackTheon Sejong 2025](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/scoreboard.webp)
_Final scoreboard. PTIT.BlueCyber is 10th with 3,764 points._

Only the top 7 got prizes. 7th place finished on 3,878, so we were 114 points short, less than a single challenge. Top 10 at an international final is a result I am happy with. But when you have seen your name in 4th and then miss by less than one solve, it is hard not to replay it, and that gap is the part that stays with you.

![The team at table 33 with our HackTheon medals](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/team-medals.webp)
_After the final, with the medals._

## From Sejong to Seoul

The awards were the next morning, July 11, and after that the trip stopped being about the scoreboard. We moved up to Seoul and met some Vietnamese friends living in Korea, and that turned into a night none of us had planned, out on the streets from evening until morning, neon everywhere, swapping stories and laughing the contest off.

That is the part of traveling with a team you cannot plan for. A trip is made as much by the random meetings and the unplanned detours as by anything you prepared in advance. The contest gave us the nerves and the regret. Seoul gave the whole thing a completely different ending.

![A night street in Seoul](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/seoul-night.webp)
_Seoul at night._

## Worth it anyway

This was also one of the last contests for PTIT.BlueCyber as a team, so it meant a bit more than a result. We flew over hoping to bring a prize home and did not, and I still wish we had held that top-7 spot in the final minutes. But a place on a scoreboard is only one part of a trip. First time abroad, a field of strong teams, hosts who took care of us, days with the team and that night in Seoul. I would take all of it again without a second thought.
