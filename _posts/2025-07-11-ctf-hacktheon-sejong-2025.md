On July 10, 2025 I sat at table 33 in a convention hall in Sejong, South Korea, with a small Vietnamese flag on a card in front of us and the name PTIT.BlueCyber printed under it. HackTheon Sejong is an international collegiate cybersecurity competition hosted by the city of Sejong and backed by the National Intelligence Service (NIS), the Ministry of Science and ICT and KISA, with a prize pool of 38 million won. Student teams from universities in Korea and abroad had qualified for the final. We finished 10th. I'm proud of that, and I'm also still a little annoyed about it, and I think both feelings are fair.

![Our table, number 33, PTIT.BlueCyber, before the final started](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/our-table.webp)
_Table 33, a few minutes before the start. Everyone got the same black case, and we'll come back to what was inside._

## Thank you, PTIT

None of this happens without the Posts and Telecommunications Institute of Technology. The school sponsored the whole trip and sent us to Korea to represent it, which for a group of students is not a small thing to be trusted with. I want to say that first, and clearly, because the result is ours but the chance to fight for it was theirs to give.

## The day

The final ran from 10:00 to 17:43, and the scoreboard graph from that day tells the story better than I can. For the first few hours we climbed. At one point we were sitting in fourth place, and I remember looking at the board and allowing myself, just for a moment, to think about the podium. That was a mistake, not because thinking about it hurt our play, but because the afternoon in a contest like this belongs to whoever still has solves left in them, and the Korean teams around us had plenty.

![The final scoreboard of HackTheon Sejong 2025](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/scoreboard.webp)
_The final board. PTIT.BlueCyber at 10th with 3,764 points, the same score as UIT.TNTCrew in 9th._

Through the afternoon we got passed, one team at a time. Every jump on someone else's line on that graph was a place we lost, and there's nothing you can do about it except keep working on the challenge in front of you. We didn't stop. Our last flag went in at 17:31:40, about twelve minutes before the end, and it pulled us up to 3,764 points.

Here's the part I still think about. UIT.TNTCrew, another Vietnamese team, finished with exactly the same 3,764. Ties are broken by who got there first, and they had made their last submission at 16:45. So they're 9th and we're 10th, separated by the clock and nothing else. Seventh and eighth place were only 114 points above us. In a contest where a single challenge is worth more than that, the gap between where we were and where we wanted to be was one solve.

That's where the regret comes from. Not from the result itself, top 10 in an international final is a good day, but from knowing exactly how close the next step was.

![The team at table 33 with our HackTheon medals](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/team-medals.webp)
_After it was over, purple shirts and medals. The screen behind us is reminding everyone to catch the 8:20 shuttle to the awards ceremony the next morning._

## Korea

The contest was one day, but the trip was more than that, and the parts I'll remember for longest aren't all on the scoreboard. The awards ceremony on the morning of the 11th, walking around a city where I couldn't read a single sign, eating whatever looked good, and then a night in Seoul wandering streets full of neon that looked like they were designed for this blog's color scheme.

![A night street in Seoul](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/seoul-night.webp)
_Achasan-ro, Seoul, a little after the contest. Tired, happy, and completely lost._

It was also one of the last competitions we played as PTIT.BlueCyber, and knowing that made the whole trip feel a bit like a goodbye. Teams change, people graduate, and you don't always notice the last time something happens. This time we did.

## What I brought home

Every finalist got the same black case, and opening it felt a bit like a loot box. Inside: the HackTheon Sejong 2025 medal, a sheet of stickers with the event mascot, a USB card, an NIS-branded gift, and my competition badge with my name and the team on it.

![The HackTheon Sejong 2025 finalist case with medal, badge and stickers](/assets/img/posts/hacktheon-sejong-2025/swag.webp)
_The finalist case: medal, badge, stickers, USB card and an NIS gift._

What I really brought home is simpler. Reaching fourth place against those teams means the level is within reach. Losing it means the afternoon matters as much as the morning: stamina, picking the right challenge when you're tired, and not getting comfortable when the board looks good. And a tie broken by a timestamp is the best argument I know for submitting the moment you have the flag.

Thank you again to PTIT for sending us. Top 10 this time. We'll see about next time.
