A year after my [consolation prize in 2022](/posts/ctf-ptit-ctf-2022/), I came back to PTIT CTF and took second prize.

The contest was the same as before, our school's own individual Jeopardy event with web, crypto, pwn and reverse engineering. What was different was me. In 2022 I entered mostly to see where I stood. This time I wanted to know whether a year of practice had actually added up to anything, and that question came with a bit of pressure I had not felt the first time.

The year in between was not a steady climb. I did not suddenly become good at RE. I still had holes in the basics and still spent a long time just working out where to start on a hard challenge. What changed was that I stopped spreading myself across every category and put most of my hours into reversing. I also learned that knowing the tools is the easy part. The harder skill is reading what the tool is actually showing you, asking the right question of it, and being patient enough to test one guess at a time instead of flailing.

I got better at failing, too. A challenge I could not solve stopped feeling like proof that I did not belong here. It just meant I had found the next thing to go learn.

![The PTIT CTF 2023 finalists with faculty members in front of the contest banner](/assets/img/posts/ptit-ctf-2023/finalists.webp)
_PTIT CTF 2023 finalists._

In the final, reversing was again where I spent most of my time, but it felt different. Less flailing, more deciding. All those scattered lab hours and all the writeups I had read to see how other people thought had quietly turned into a way of working. Progress, I think, is less about instantly cracking something unfamiliar and more about knowing where to start, what to look up, and which ideas to drop early.

![Award winners of PTIT CTF 2023 on stage](/assets/img/posts/ptit-ctf-2023/awards-stage.webp)
_The awards ceremony, October 2, 2023._

The awards were handed out on October 2, 2023 in hall A2, on the same day the faculty welcomed its new students, so the place was packed. Going from consolation to second prize in twelve months felt good, mostly because it tied the result back to the work. The practice was doing something.

Second prize did not make me an expert, and plenty of the contest was still hard. But where 2022 was the year I found a direction, 2023 was the year I started to trust it.

A couple of months later I played my first Attack-Defense final at the ASEAN Student Contest, which turned out to be a completely different kind of pressure. I wrote about that [here](/posts/ctf-asean-2023-first-attack-defense/).
