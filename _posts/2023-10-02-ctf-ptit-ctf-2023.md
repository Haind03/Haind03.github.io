PTIT CTF is our school's own contest, the Student Information Security Competition run by the Faculty of Information Security at the Posts and Telecommunications Institute of Technology. It's individual Jeopardy, covering web, crypto, pwn and reverse engineering, and for a lot of us at ISP Club it's the first contest we take seriously. On October 2, 2023, in hall A2, the faculty held the awards ceremony for the 2023 edition together with its welcome event for new students. I walked up on that stage with a second prize.

_The PTIT CTF 2023 final round. Finalists and faculty members in front of the contest banner._

## One year on

The year before, in 2022, I'd played the same contest and finished with [a consolation prize](/posts/ctf-ptit-ctf-2022/). That result told me two things: that I could hang with the people in the final, and that I was nowhere near the top. So the year in between was a lot of practice, most of it in reversing, which is where I spent the 2023 final as well. Moving from consolation to second prize in the same contest a year later is the clearest measure of progress I had at that point, and honestly it meant more to me than the prize itself.

Individual Jeopardy is a lonely format compared to team contests. There's nobody to hand a challenge to when you're stuck, nobody to split categories with, and every wrong turn costs your own time. You find out quickly which categories you're actually good at and which ones you've only been good at with teammates around.

## The ceremony

It was a good result for ISP Club as a whole. A clubmate took first prize, and I shared second prize with two other club members, so four of the top places went to people who'd been training together all year. Holding a certificate and a bunch of flowers on stage at our own school, in front of the new students who had just joined the faculty that same day, is a nice kind of pressure. Some of them were probably sitting in that hall wondering whether they'd be up there one day.
